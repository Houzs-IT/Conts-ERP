The Conts Digital Brand Guide
Property Management System and Owner Portal
Version 1.0  |  16 September 2026
Prepared for The Conts product design and engineering teams
Purpose
This guide translates The Conts existing premium property-management identity into a consistent, scalable digital product system. It is the working specification for Claude Design, Figma, frontend implementation, and future product QA.
Brand basis
Public-facing materials consistently support a black, white and champagne-gold architectural direction. Exact official logo geometry, master color values and legacy font licenses were not publicly available; the values marked Provisional below are implementation recommendations pending verification against original brand assets.

4.3 Application rules
Gold communicates brand, selection and premium emphasis. It must never mean success, warning or error.
Primary destructive actions remain red. Confirmation actions may use black or a context-appropriate semantic color.
Use a maximum of one dominant accent per view.
Never communicate status by color alone; pair color with text, icon or shape.
Body text and interactive controls must meet WCAG AA contrast. Large display text does not exempt operational data from readability.
4.4 Dark mode
Do not launch a full dark mode by simply inverting the palette. Treat dark mode as a separate tested theme. The initial release should use a dark navigation shell and warm-light workspace; this retains brand impact while supporting long work sessions and dense tables.

5.3 Numeric typography
Enable tabular numerals for financial values, dates, occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, SLAs and table columns.

Avoid all-caps operational messages.
Use concise nouns for navigation and verb-first language for actions: Add property, Assign cleaner, Resolve issue.

12 Data visualisation
Use direct labels where possible and keep legends close to charts.
Reserve gold for The Conts or the selected series. Comparison series use charcoal and neutral grey.
Use semantic green and red only when direction is genuinely good or bad. Revenue decline is not automatically red if it is expected seasonality.
Every chart needs timeframe, unit, source or freshness indicator, and an accessible table or summary.
Avoid 3D charts, decorative gradients, gauges and more than five simultaneous categorical colours.

13.1 Navigation rules
Organise around jobs and decisions, never raw database tables.
Keep the current building, unit, owner or date context visible when moving between related pages.
Use breadcrumbs for deep entity pages; do not duplicate the page title in every navigation layer.
Global search should find units, bookings, guests, owners, tasks and transaction references.
Permission restrictions should remove inaccessible destinations or clearly explain access; do not tease unusable actions.
14 Screen patterns
14.1 Management dashboard
Top row: Revenue, Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR and Net Owner Payout with comparison periods.
Primary visual: revenue and occupancy trend with selectable timeframe.
Today panel: check-ins, check-outs, cleaning pending, maintenance issues and guest escalations.
Portfolio table: property, revenue, occupancy, ADR, rating and exception status.
Default emphasis is business health and exceptions, not decorative property imagery.
14.2 Operations today
Start with overdue and check-in-risk items.
Group by operational urgency, then time; do not force a decorative Kanban if a prioritized list is faster.
Each item shows unit, issue, next guest time, assignee, SLA and one clear action.
Provide shift and building filters plus a role-appropriate mobile view.
14.3 Property and unit record
Header: property, unit, live status and active channels.
Tabs: Overview, Reservations, Operations, Financials, Maintenance, Documents and Activity.
Overview combines current guest, next check-in, readiness, open issues, owner and monthly P and L.
Activity is chronological, attributable and filterable.
14.4 Reservation calendar
Rows represent units; columns represent dates; booking blocks show channel and status.
Differentiate Airbnb, Booking.com, Agoda, direct booking, maintenance block and owner stay without relying on color alone.
Prevent accidental drag changes with confirmation and conflict feedback.
Keep unit names frozen while horizontal scrolling.

17 Accessibility and localisation
Target WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast, keyboard use, focus visibility, error identification and names of controls.
Visible focus ring: 2 px minimum with offset; choose black or high-contrast gold based on surface.
Minimum touch target 44 by 44 px in field and owner experiences.
Do not rely on placeholder text, color, icons or hover alone.
Support English first, with architecture ready for Bahasa Malaysia and Simplified Chinese.
Allow at least 30 percent text expansion without clipping.
Dates: use 16 Sep 2026 in compact English contexts; always disambiguate date ranges.
Timezone: Asia Kuala Lumpur. Currency: MYR with RM presentation.
Do not encode meaning in flags; language and market are separate concepts.

19 Security trust and audit cues
Financial, identity, access-code and owner data require role-based access and least privilege.
Sensitive values should be masked by default where full display is not necessary.
Destructive and financial actions show target, amount, consequence and actor.

21.4 Engineering rules
Confirm the exact current Houzs and Hookka package stack before implementation; do not replace libraries for aesthetic reasons.
Prefer composition and variants over copying shared components into a The Conts folder.
A brand change must require token changes, not edits across pages.
A shared behavior fix should be released to all three systems through the same source package or monorepo workspace.
Product-specific components may be promoted to shared patterns only after their API is stable.
Keep one Storybook or equivalent component catalogue showing Houzs, Hookka and The Conts themes side by side.
Add visual regression tests for all shared components under each theme.
Document any unavoidable fork with an owner, reason and removal condition.

22 Claude Design master brief
Copy the following specification into Claude Design together with the product requirements for each screen.
Design a complete Property Management System and Owner Portal for The Conts Property Management in Malaysia. Strictly extend the existing The Conts identity: premium architectural black, white and champagne gold. Do not create a new generic SaaS brand and do not imitate Airbnb.
Position the product as The Conts Property OS: a calm, precise and operational platform connecting properties, owners, guests, reservations, revenue, pricing, housekeeping, maintenance, customer service, payments, staff and analytics.
Use a deep black or charcoal navigation shell, a warm-white workspace, white primary surfaces, warm-neutral borders and champagne gold only for brand identity, selection and restrained premium emphasis. Gold must not represent success, warning or errors. Use green, amber, red, blue and neutral grey only for semantic operational states. Status must never depend on color alone.
Use the exact same application font, React frontend foundation, component library, icon library, form patterns, tables, charts and interaction conventions already used by Houzs ERP and Hookka ERP. Do not invent a separate frontend system for The Conts. Apply The Conts identity through semantic theme tokens and PMS-specific composition. Use sentence case, tabular numerals, right-aligned financial columns and strong but restrained numerical hierarchy. Default body text is 14 px. Use the shared spacing, radius and elevation primitives; introduce a new token only when the existing shared system cannot express a documented requirement.
Design desktop-first for managers and operational staff, with responsive tablet and mobile workflows for cleaners and technicians. The owner portal must be simpler, lower-density and focused on earnings, deductions, payout, performance and property health.
Every screen must answer: What is happening? What needs attention? What action should I take next? Surface overdue, blocked and check-in-risk work before routine work. Keep context connected across units, bookings, operations and finance.
Map Figma components one to one to the existing shared React components for buttons, inputs, selects, date controls, badges, cards, tables, tabs, dialogs, toasts, loading, empty states and charts. Preserve current component APIs and states. New PMS components should be compositions of shared primitives unless a genuine domain interaction requires a new component. Target WCAG 2.2 AA and 44 px touch targets for field workflows.
Main navigation: Overview; Reservations; Operations; Properties; Finance; Owners; Performance; Communication; System. Organize by user workflow, never database structure.
The result must feel like a premium property operations product: information-dense, highly usable, financially transparent and unmistakably The Conts. Avoid excessive gradients, glassmorphism, decorative gold, oversized cards, playful illustration, generic blue SaaS styling and ornamental luxury that slows daily work.
